UCU Mukono Accessibility Audit

Identifying barriers and suggesting practical improvements

Introduction Issues Solutions Conclusion

Introduction

This accessibility audit looks at some barriers that may affect students, staff and visitors at Uganda Christian University (UCU), Mukono. The aim is to identify the people affected, explain the barriers and suggest practical improvements.

Identified Issues

1. Accessibility for Students with Disabilities

Who may be affected: Students with disabilities, staff and visitors.

Barrier: Some buildings and pathways may be difficult to access for people with mobility challenges.

Improvement: Provide more ramps, accessible toilets, clear signs and improved pathways.

2. Internet and Digital Access

Who may be affected: Students and lecturers.

Barrier: Poor or unreliable connectivity can make it difficult to access online learning materials and submit work.

Improvement: Improve Wi-Fi coverage and network capacity around learning and study areas.

3. Administrative Services

Who may be affected: Students and administrative staff.

Barrier: Students may experience difficulties when accessing different administrative services.

Improvement: Create a central student-service system where students can access services and track requests.

4. Library and Study Spaces

Who may be affected: Students and researchers.

Barrier: During busy academic periods, students may need more convenient and quiet study spaces.

Improvement: Increase study spaces, improve room booking and provide more charging points and computers.

5. Roads and Walkways

Who may be affected: Students, staff, visitors and people with mobility difficulties.

Barrier: Poor or difficult-to-navigate walkways can make movement around campus challenging.

Improvement: Improve pedestrian paths, drainage, lighting and directional signs.

Recommended Improvements

  • Improve physical accessibility across campus.
  • Expand reliable internet coverage.
  • Improve digital student services.
  • Increase accessible study facilities.
  • Improve campus walkways, lighting and signs.

Conclusion

Improving accessibility can make the university environment easier to use and more inclusive for students, staff and visitors. The recommended solutions focus on practical improvements that can support learning, movement and access to university services.
